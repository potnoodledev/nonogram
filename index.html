How to play

Each row and column is labelled with the lengths of its runs of filled squares, in order. Runs are separated by at least one blank square.

For example, a clue of 2 1 means two filled squares, then a gap, then one filled square. The clue does not say where in the line they start — the crossing clues narrow that down.

  • Click or tap a square to fill it.
  • Use Mark mode or right-click to cross out a blank.
  • Drag across squares to paint a whole line at once.
  • Check briefly highlights squares that clash with the solution.
Nonogram tips
  • Start with the longest clues: a run as long as the line fills the whole line.
  • A completed row or column turns its numbers teal, so you can see the progress.
  • Every puzzle here has one solution and can be solved by logic alone.
  • Your progress is saved in this browser automatically.
How to play

Fill the 9×9 grid so every row, every column and every 3×3 box contains the digits 1–9 exactly once. The printed numbers are fixed.

  • Select a cell, then click a number or press 1–9.
  • Press Backspace or Delete, or right-click, to clear a cell.
  • Selecting a cell highlights its row, column and box.
  • Check briefly highlights digits that clash with the solution.
Sudoku tips
  • Start with the rows, columns or boxes that already hold the most numbers.
  • A digit can only appear once in each row, column and box.
  • Every puzzle here has exactly one solution and can be solved by logic alone.
  • Your progress is saved in this browser automatically.
How to play

Place stars so every row, every column and every outlined region holds the same number of stars: one on the 6×6 and 8×8 boards, two on the 10×10 board. Two stars never touch, not even at a corner.

  • Click or tap a cell to cycle from empty to star to dot and back.
  • Drag across cells to dot a whole run at once.
  • Use right-click to add or remove a dot without touching a star.
  • Check briefly highlights cells that clash with the solution; clashing stars turn red as you play.
Star Battle tips
  • Start with a region that has only as many cells as it needs stars: they all hold stars.
  • A row or column with just enough free cells for its missing stars works the same way.
  • Once a star is placed, dot the eight cells around it.
  • Every puzzle here has exactly one solution and can be solved by logic alone.
  • Your progress is saved in this browser automatically.
